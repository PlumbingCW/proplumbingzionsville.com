What counts as a plumbing emergency.

Some problems cannot wait for office hours, and they are the reason to call an emergency plumber. Water that will not stop, sewage rising in a floor drain, or no water at all on a freezing night all belong here. A split supply line keeps running until someone closes a valve, and the water finds drywall, insulation and outlets fast.

A gas smell is a different case. Leave the building first, then call the gas utility or 911 from outside, and skip the light switch on the way out. Call us once the house is cleared.

A dripping faucet, a slow tub drain or a toilet that runs after flushing is a real problem but rarely an emergency. Those fit a planned visit, and a same-day slot may be open. If you are unsure which kind you have, call and we will sort it out with you.

What to do first while you wait.

Five steps that limit damage before the plumber reaches your door.

These steps follow the Indiana Office of Utility Consumer Counselor guidelines on frozen and burst pipes.

1

Close the main shutoff.

Find the valve where the water line enters the house and close it all the way.

2

Cut power to the water heater.

If the tank or its piping leaks, switch off its breaker or gas control so a half-empty tank does not keep heating.

3

Open a low faucet.

With the main closed, a tap on the lowest floor drains the line and relieves pressure.

4

Photograph everything.

Take pictures of standing water, wet materials and the failed part before cleanup, for your insurance claim.

5

Stay out of water near outlets.

Do not wade toward the panel or outlets. Use the breaker only if you can reach it while dry.
