When a burst pipe is an emergency.

Almost every burst pipe is urgent, because supply lines run at household pressure and keep pouring until the valve is closed. A split pipe can release many gallons in minutes, soaking drywall, insulation and flooring. Water near a panel or outlets adds an electrical risk.

Call the emergency line at any hour, including nights and weekends, and close the main valve first. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. A small pinhole spray can wait for a morning visit once the water is off.

What to do while you wait.

Do these in order. Each one takes a few minutes at most.

1

Close the main shutoff.

It sits where the water line enters the house, often in a basement or crawl space. Turn it fully closed, and find it ahead of time if you can.

2

Open taps to drain the lines.

Open a faucet at the lowest level and one upstairs to relieve pressure and let the pipes empty.

3

Turn off the water heater.

An electric element can burn out if the tank drains. Switch off its breaker or set a gas unit to pilot.

4

Kill power to wet areas.

If water reaches outlets or the panel, shut off those breakers from a dry spot only.

5

Protect and photograph.

Move rugs and electronics, soak up what you can, and take dated photos.

Why pipes burst in Zionsville homes.

Water expands about 9 percent when it freezes, and a sealed pipe cannot absorb that. The split usually appears at a weak point, often where the ice blocked the line, and shows once things thaw. Indianapolis normals from NWS (1991-2020) put the January average low at 20.9 F, and the record low is -27 F from January 19, 1994.

Not every burst comes from cold. Corroded copper, thin galvanized lines, a failed fitting or a nail through a pipe cause failures in any season. NWS lists Indianapolis's average first freeze as October 26 and last as April 17, so exterior walls, garages and crawl spaces are at risk from late autumn into spring.

Signs of a burst or cracked pipe.

Some bursts are loud. Others are only a stain and a rising bill.

Water where it should not be.

A wet ceiling, a puddle under a wall or water coming through a light fixture usually means a split line above.

No water or a weak flow.

If one or more taps run dry or sputter, a frozen or broken line may be blocking flow.

Meter running with taps closed.

A moving meter after the house valve is closed points to a break outside the house.

Frost, bulge or crack on a pipe.

Exposed pipe that is frosted, bowed or split along a seam has been through a freeze.

Cracked and broken pipe repair.

A cracked pipe can be a hairline seam in copper, a split fitting or a fractured section of galvanized steel. It may spray or just seep, and the metal around it is often weakened too.

We close the supply, drain the section, and cut out the damaged length back to sound pipe. A new piece goes in with fittings to suit the existing line, whether copper, PEX or CPVC. Then we restore pressure slowly and watch the joint, because trapped air can hammer a fresh joint.

A break behind a wall or under a floor gets found first. Our leak detection page describes how listening gear and pressure tests place it before anything is cut. Drywall patching and painting afterward are separate trades.

One crack on a sound line is a repair. Several cracks, or pipe that crumbles when handled, point to whole-line repiping instead.

Copper, drain and supply pipe repair.

Copper pipe repair is the most common burst job. We cut out the section, sweat in a new piece or use press fittings, and test the joint under pressure before the wall is closed.

Drain pipe repair differs because the pipe is not pressurized. Cracked drain lines leak when water flows, cause damp spots or odors, and must keep their slope and vent connection when replaced. If the crack is in the sewer line outside the house, see sewer line repair.

Pipe leak repair covers small failures such as a pinhole, a weeping joint or a loose compression fitting. We say so when a smaller repair will hold.

Frozen pipe repair.

A frozen pipe has not always burst yet. Ice blocks the flow, and the pressure behind it can crack the pipe. If a tap runs dry in cold weather, treat it as a freeze. The Indiana OUCC says to shut off water at the main valve right away and call a licensed plumber.

Never use a torch or open flame, which risks fire and can melt solder joints. After thawing, the OUCC says to turn the water back on slowly and watch for leaks, since a hairline crack can start dripping hours later.

We thaw the line safely, check it, and replace sections that failed. Then we look at why it froze, such as an uninsulated wall, a drafty crawl space or a hose left connected.

Preventing frozen pipes.

What affects the price of burst pipe repair.

These factors decide the final number.

Pipe location.

A split under a sink is quick. A break in a wall, ceiling or slab takes more access work.

Pipe material.

Copper, PEX, CPVC and galvanized each need their own parts and methods.

Length of damage.

One crack is a small repair. A long frozen run may need a whole section replaced.

After-hours timing.

Nights, weekends and holidays can change scheduling and rates, and we tell you before work starts.

Restoration.

Patching drywall, flooring or insulation is usually a separate cost.
