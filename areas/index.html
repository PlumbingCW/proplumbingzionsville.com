Zionsville, Indiana (ZIP 46077).

Zionsville is a Boone County town of about 33,600 residents (Census Bureau estimate for July 2025), and 84.2 percent of its homes are owner-occupied. That means the homeowner usually makes the repair decision, so we explain the price to the person who pays it.

The Town runs its own sewer service and bills a flat monthly rate per residence. If sewage backs up inside your house, a plumber checks your own drain line first, and the Town can tell you about its mains in the street.

Zionsville places and what they mean for your call.

Landmarks make good directions when you call at night. Each one below also has a practical side.

  • Lincoln Park Abraham Lincoln spoke at Zionsville's original depot here in 1861. Today it is an easy landmark for downtown addresses when you give us directions.

Before anyone digs.

Repairing a buried water or sewer line means digging. Indiana 811 must receive a notice at least two full working days before digging, and no more than ten calendar days before. Utility operators then mark their lines. Tell us if a yard repair is likely so the timing is clear from the start.

Whitestown, Indiana (ZIP 46075).

Whitestown is a neighbouring Boone County town on our regular route. The Whitestown plumbing page covers how calls work there. Census counts rose from 2,867 residents in 2010 to 10,178 in 2020, which suggests many newer homes with newer plumbing.

The town's utilities department provides sewer and stormwater service, and its billing office is at 6320 South Cozy Lane. When a drain backs up, the first question is whether the blockage sits in your own line or in the town's. Call, and we help you sort it out.
