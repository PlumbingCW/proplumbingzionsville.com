When a sewer line failure is an emergency.

Treat it as an emergency when wastewater is coming up through a floor drain, a tub or a toilet in more than one place. That pattern points past a single clogged fixture to the main line. Every flush and every load of laundry pushes more water into a pipe that cannot carry it away.

Raw sewage in the house is also a health problem. Keep children and pets out of the area, do not touch it bare-handed, and do not run a shop vacuum through it. Call us at any hour, including nights, weekends and holidays, and we will tell you what to shut off while a plumber is on the way.

What to do while you wait.

Five quick moves keep a bad backup from getting worse.

1

Stop all water use.

No flushing, showers, dishwasher or washing machine. Each gallon you send down has nowhere to go but back into the house.

2

Find the cleanout.

A capped pipe, usually 4 inches wide, sits in the basement, crawl space or yard near the house. Do not open it unless we ask, since pressure behind the cap can spill sewage.

3

Move belongings off the floor.

Lift rugs, boxes and electronics away from the affected drains. Leave the electrical panel alone if water is near it.

4

Photograph everything.

Take pictures of the water line, damaged items and the drains before cleanup. Your insurance adjuster will want them.

5

Open a window and keep people out.

Sewer gas smells bad and the water carries bacteria. Ventilate if you can and keep the area closed off.

Why sewer lines fail in Zionsville homes.

With that much invested in a house, a lateral that fails is worth repairing correctly the first time.

Most failures come from three things. Roots enter through joints looking for moisture. Older clay and cast iron pipe cracks or flakes with age. Soil settles and leaves a low spot, which the plumbing trade calls a belly, where solids sit and clog the line over and over.

Signs your sewer line needs repair, not just cleaning.

A clog that a drain machine clears for good is a clog. These patterns suggest the pipe itself is damaged.

It clogs again within weeks.

A line cleared by cable that backs up again a few weeks later often has a root mass or a sag holding debris.

Several drains gurgle together.

Air pushed out of a blocked main bubbles up through the nearest open drain, often a floor drain or tub.

Sewage smell in the yard.

A wet, foul patch or a stretch of unusually green grass over the line suggests wastewater leaking into the soil.

A sunken spot in the lawn.

Soil washing into a broken pipe leaves a void, and the ground settles above it.

Slow drains everywhere.

When the kitchen sink, tub and washer all drain slowly together, the shared line is the likely cause.

Cleaning keeps hitting a wall.

A cable that stops at the same spot every time points to a collapse, an offset joint or a root ball.

How we find the break before we dig.

We run a waterproof camera head on a push cable through the cleanout. The monitor shows cracks, offset joints, roots, scale and standing water, and we record the run. If the line is full of debris, we clear it first, since a camera cannot see through grease and paper.

A transmitter in the camera head lets a locator above ground read the depth and position of the fault. That tells us how much yard, driveway or slab sits over the problem before anyone picks up a shovel. If you only want the pipe examined, our sewer camera inspection service covers that on its own.

Sewer backup in the house.

A sewer backup happens when wastewater cannot leave the house at the rate it enters. The cause is either a blockage the pipe is able to clear, or a pipe that is broken. A main clog from grease or paper can often be cleared with a cable or water jet, and our page on sewer line cleaning covers that route.

If the line backs up again soon after cleaning, or the camera finds a break, cleaning will not hold. Then repair is the real fix. We look at the line either way, because clearing a pipe without looking inside it can leave a crack or a root mass for the next backup.

Before cleanup starts, document the water level, the rooms affected and what was damaged. Standard homeowner policies often cover sudden and accidental water damage but may treat a backup from a sewer or drain as a separate endorsement. Check your policy and ask your agent.

Trenchless pipe lining, and where it stops working.

Lining works like this. A felt tube soaked in epoxy resin is pulled into the cleaned pipe, inflated against the walls and left to harden into a pipe inside the old pipe. It seals cracks and joints and blocks roots from entering again, with only small access holes needed.

The liner follows the shape of the old pipe, so it cannot fix a sag or bridge a missing section. It also makes the inside diameter slightly smaller. We only recommend it when the camera shows a pipe that is still round and mostly intact, and we say so plainly when excavation is the better answer.

Cast iron sewer pipe.

Many older houses have cast iron drain and sewer pipe inside the foundation or under the slab. Iron rusts from the inside, building rough scale that catches paper and grease. Eventually the wall thins, cracks or splits along the bottom.

Camera work shows how bad the scale is. Light buildup can be cleaned. A cracked or flaking section can be cut out and replaced with plastic pipe, and a long run in poor condition may call for a larger job. If a slab has to be opened, we explain that before the work is scheduled.

Septic systems and what we do not do.

Most Zionsville homes connect to the Town's sewer, but a house on a private septic system works differently. Indiana local health departments issue septic permits, and the state lists residential rule 410 IAC 6-8.3 for onsite systems. For septic permits or design questions, contact the Boone County Health Department.

We do not pump septic tanks or design drain fields. We can camera the pipe between your house and the tank, find a root-damaged or broken line, and repair it. If the real problem is a full tank or a failing field, we will tell you to call a septic service instead of selling you a pipe repair.

Who owns which part of the sewer line.

The sewer main under the street belongs to the utility. The lateral, the pipe running from your house to that main, is usually the property owner's. Exact boundaries vary by address and by Town ordinance, so before you commit to a repair, ask the Town of Zionsville where responsibility ends for your lot. Your wastewater billing account is the right place to start.

Digging in Indiana starts with a call. Indiana 811 must receive the excavation notice at least two full working days ahead and no more than ten calendar days ahead, and a ticket expires 20 days after submission. If your repair needs a trench, we plan the schedule around that window. For permits, ask the Zionsville Planning & Building Department, since rules differ by project.

What affects the price of sewer line repair.

Sewer work ranges from a short spot repair to a full replacement, so no single figure applies. These are the factors that move a real quote.

Method.

Lining, bursting and open trench cost different amounts and need different equipment and crew time.

Length and depth.

A longer or deeper run takes more excavation, more pipe and more time before the yard can be restored.

What sits on top.

Lawn is the simplest. A driveway, patio, tree or slab adds cutting, hauling and repair work.

Pipe condition.

Heavy scale, roots or a collapse can mean cleaning and extra pipe before the main repair starts.

Access and time of day.

A clear cleanout and open ground help. Night and weekend emergency work is staffed differently from a scheduled day visit.

A burst pipe that floods a room is the kind of sudden, accidental loss policies tend to cover. A cracked lateral that has leaked for months, or the cost of the pipe itself, usually is not covered.
