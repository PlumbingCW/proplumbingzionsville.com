When a clogged drain is an emergency.

Most clogs are a nuisance. A few are not. Water rising in a tub or floor drain with nothing running is one sign. A toilet and a shower that both bubble when you drain the sink is another. Either way the blockage sits in a shared line, so call at any hour.

Dirty water that reaches the floor is a health problem as well as a mess. Keep children and pets out of it, switch off power to anything plugged in near standing water, and do not use the fixtures downstream of the clog. Every flush or load of laundry adds gallons to a line that has nowhere to go.

What to do while you wait.

These steps take about ten minutes and make our visit shorter. None of them need tools beyond a bucket and a flashlight.

1

Stop the water.

Pause the dishwasher, washing machine and showers. A standard washer empties 15 to 30 gallons in one drain cycle, which is plenty to flood a floor drain.

2

Find out how far it reaches.

Run a single faucet for a few seconds on each level. Note which fixtures drain slowly. A clog in one sink points to its own branch, while several fixtures point to the main.

3

Locate the cleanout.

Look for a threaded cap near the floor, usually in a basement, garage wall or crawl space, or outdoors near the foundation. Do not open it if water is already standing in it.

4

Move belongings.

Lift rugs, cardboard boxes and anything stored near a floor drain. Water damage from drain overflow is hard to dry out once it wicks into cardboard or carpet pad.

5

Photograph the problem.

Take clear pictures of standing water and any damage with the date showing on your phone. Your insurance agent will want them if the overflow reaches finished surfaces.

Signs a drain is clogging before it stops.

Drains rarely fail all at once. They tell you weeks ahead, and a sink that was cleared once and slows again a few weeks later usually has residue still clinging to the pipe wall.

Slow draining.

Water leaves the sink or tub in a lazy swirl instead of a steady pull. A narrowed pipe passes less water per second.

Gurgling.

Air pulled through a water seal makes a bubbling sound when another fixture drains. It means the line is partly blocked or the vent is struggling.

Sewage-like smell.

A foul odor at one drain often means debris trapped just past the P-trap. A smell in a floor drain points toward the main.

Water at the lowest drain.

When the shower floods during a laundry load, the shared line is carrying more than the blockage allows through.

Repeat clogs.

If a drain clears and clogs again within weeks, the first pass probably only poked a hole through the blockage rather than removing it.

Fruit flies at a drain.

Small flies hovering over one drain can mean a film of organic matter on the pipe wall that a quick snake pass left behind.

Why drains clog in Zionsville homes.

The Census Bureau reports a median owner-occupied home value of $587,800 in Zionsville and an owner-occupied rate of 84.2 percent. Larger family homes tend to have more bathrooms, a laundry room and a basement bath, and every added fixture adds a branch line that can collect hair, soap scum and lint.

Winter makes kitchen lines worse. The January normal low at Indianapolis is 20.9 F, and a drain that runs along an exterior wall or through an unheated crawl space cools quickly. Warm cooking grease that slides down in the evening can set up on cold pipe walls and build a ring that narrows the pipe over the season.

How we find and clear the clog.

We start by asking which fixtures are slow and how long it has been happening, then test each drain. That tells us whether the blockage sits in the trap, in a branch line or in the main. A small auger handles a trap. A drum cable machine with a 1/4 to 3/8 inch cable fits most sink, tub and shower lines.

Larger 3 and 4 inch lines take a heavier sectional cable with a cutting head matched to the blockage, whether that is hair, paper, grease or a root. When the cable meets something we cannot identify by feel, we run a drain camera to see it, and that camera view also shows whether the pipe is cracked or offset. Where a line keeps coming back, hydro jetting may be the better tool.

Kitchen sink drain clogs.

The kitchen line is different from a bathroom line because of what goes into it. Cooking fat, coffee grounds, rice and pasta water all leave residue. Fat cools in the pipe and sticks to the wall, then catches food scraps, and the narrowing continues until the sink holds water. Kitchen drains are usually 1.5 inches wide, so even a thin ring of grease matters.

If both sink basins back up together, the clog is past the tailpiece and trap, so look at the disposal and the branch beyond it. A jammed garbage disposal can mimic a drain clog. We check the disposal first, then cable the line from the trap arm or a cleanout, and we pull out what we find so you can see it.

Drain snaking and cabling, and what each tool does.

Snaking is the common name for running a flexible steel cable through a drain. The cable turns as it advances, and the head either hooks the blockage and pulls it back or bores a hole through it. Hair at a tub drain usually comes out on the hook. A grease plug is more stubborn and may need a blade head and a second pass.

A short cable only reaches so far. A 20-foot snake from a hardware store often stops at the first bend or well before the main line. Our machines carry longer cable on a drum, which lets us reach the main from a basement cleanout or a roof vent when needed. Each machine has a limit, and past it a camera or a jet is the right next step.

Clogged drain, unclog options you can try first.

A flat plunger with a good seal clears many tub and sink clogs. Cover the overflow opening with a wet cloth so pressure goes down the pipe, and work it for about twenty strokes. Pull hair out of a tub drain with a plastic barbed strip. These are safe and cost almost nothing.

We suggest staying away from liquid drain openers. They are caustic, they sit in a standing-water clog and can damage some pipe materials, and they are a chemical hazard for whoever opens the trap next. Never mix two products. If you already poured one in, tell us before we start so we can wear the right gloves and eye protection.

Drain line leaks and other drain jobs.

Not every wet floor under a sink is a clog. A cracked trap, a loose slip nut or a corroded tailpiece leaks only when water is running. If your cabinet stays dry until you drain the basin, the leak is on the drain side, and we fix it during the same visit as the clog.

We also clear floor drains, laundry standpipes and tub drains, and we can open a roof vent when a blocked vent is the reason a fixture gurgles. Larger main-line backups are covered on our sewer line cleaning page.
