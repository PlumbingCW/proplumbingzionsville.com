Sewer Camera Inspection in Zionsville, IN.

A sewer camera inspection in Zionsville, IN is a look inside your drain line. A waterproof camera on a flexible cable goes in through a cleanout and runs until we have seen the whole line to the street or the tank. The monitor shows the pipe material, the joints and any roots, cracks, sags or scale, and we record the video so you can see it too.

People book it before buying a house, after the same drain keeps clogging, or before putting in a patio or tree. The goal is simple. Decide what to repair, if anything, from what is actually in the pipe and not from a guess.

What the camera can and cannot tell you.

The camera head carries its own lights and a small lens, on a long push cable that reaches most house-to-street runs. It shows whether the pipe is clay, cast iron, plastic or something else. It shows whether joints line up, whether roots have found a seam, and whether water is sitting where it should flow.

It cannot see through standing water or heavy buildup, so a dirty line may need cleaning first. It also cannot tell you who owns a section of pipe or whether a repair needs a permit. Those questions go to the Town of Zionsville and the Planning & Building Department.

How a camera visit works.

The visit is short and you can watch most of it.

1

Find the access point.

We use the cleanout, a pulled toilet or a roof vent when no cleanout exists. A cleanout is the capped pipe, often 4 inches wide, at the base of the line.

2

Run the camera.

The head travels down the pipe while a counter on the cable tracks distance. We stop at anything unusual and note how far it is from the house.

3

Locate from above.

A transmitter inside the camera head lets a handheld receiver find the same spot at the surface and read its approximate depth.

4

Review the video together.

We play back the problem spots, explain what each one is, and say which are cosmetic and which need action.

5

Decide on next steps.

Nothing found means nothing to fix. If something is found, we explain the repair options on that pipe, not a generic sales list.

What we look for and what it means.

These are the usual findings on a camera run.

Roots.

Fine hair-like roots in a joint are common. A thick mass that blocks half the pipe is a different problem and usually means a repair.

Offset joints.

Two pipe sections that no longer line up leave a lip that catches paper and lets soil in.

Bellies.

A low spot holds water along the pipe. Solids settle in it, and the line clogs repeatedly.

Cracks and breaks.

A longitudinal crack may hold for a while. A circular break or missing section can let the pipe collapse.

Scale and corrosion.

Rough, flaky buildup in cast iron narrows the pipe and traps debris.

Grease and debris.

A coated pipe is usually a maintenance issue, not a structural one, and cleaning may solve it.

What a recording proves, and what it does not.

A video is strong evidence, but it has limits.

What it proves.

  • The pipe's condition on the day of the run.
  • Where a defect sits and about how deep.
  • Whether the cause of repeat clogs is structural.
  • A baseline for comparison later.

What it does not prove.

  • How long the problem has been there.
  • Which lines the Town versus the owner must maintain.
  • A section hidden behind standing water or debris.
  • That a repair will not need a permit.
