Emergency Sewer Line Cleaning in Zionsville, IN.

Sewer line cleaning in Zionsville, IN is the clearing of the large pipe that carries every drain in your house out to the sewer. That pipe, usually 4 inches across, runs from the lowest fixtures to the street. When it blocks, the lowest drain in the house is where the water shows up first, often a basement floor drain or a tub.

Backups often start late in the evening, and our 24/7 emergency line takes them any hour. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows. Until we get there, stop flushing and running water, because each gallon goes to the lowest opening in the house. We also serve nearby towns, including Whitestown.

When a sewer backup is an emergency.

Sewage coming up through a floor drain, a tub or a basement toilet is an emergency. Wastewater from a main line backup carries bacteria, and it can soak drywall, carpet and stored boxes within an hour. Call right away, at any hour.

Do not flush to see if it clears. Do not run the washer. If water reaches an outlet, a freezer or a furnace, stay out of that area. Shut off power at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water. Otherwise ask us or your electric utility how to proceed.

What to do while you wait for sewer cleaning.

Your goal is to keep the volume down and the damage small. These steps help in that order.

1

Stop flushing.

A toilet sends 1.6 gallons or more down the line with each flush, and the line is already full. Use a bucket and a different bathroom if one is upstairs.

2

Hold the laundry.

A washer discharges 15 to 30 gallons in a few minutes, enough to push a backup across a basement floor.

3

Check neighbors.

If other homes on your street have the same problem, the blockage may be in the town main. Call the Town's wastewater office, since we do not work on municipal mains.

4

Open the cleanout cap carefully.

If the cleanout is dry, a loosened cap can relieve pressure. If water is rising inside it, leave it closed and wait for us.

5

Document everything.

Photograph the water line on walls and the items affected, and keep damaged goods until your adjuster has seen them.

Sewer backup, what is happening in the pipe.

A sewer backup means the main line cannot pass water as fast as the house sends it. Water takes the nearest way out, which is the lowest drain. A partial clog gives slow drains and gurgling. A full clog gives backups. Both come from the same causes, which are grease, wipes, paper, roots, or a section of pipe that has shifted.

The first clue is which fixtures react. If only the basement tub and floor drain fill while the upstairs toilets flush, the blockage is downstream of the lowest branch, in the main. If one bathroom backs up alone, the problem is in that branch and drain cleaning may be all it needs.

We find out in one visit. We open the main cleanout, feed a cable, and see where it stops. The distance tells us roughly where the blockage sits, and whether it is inside the house or out toward the street.

Signs the main line is the problem.

A main line seldom blocks without warning. Look for these patterns, especially if more than one appears.

Several drains slow at once.

Sinks, tubs and the washer all drain slowly together, which points to the shared line.

Gurgling toilets.

A toilet bubbles when the washer drains because air is being forced back through the water seals.

Water in the lowest drain.

Water pools in a basement floor drain or shower while you use fixtures upstairs.

Sewer odor.

A steady smell of sewage inside the house or near the cleanout suggests the line is holding waste.

Wet or lush spots in the yard.

A soggy patch or unusually green grass over the line path can mean a break, which cleaning alone will not fix.

Repeat backups.

A line that fills again a few weeks after clearing has roots, a belly or damage, and needs a camera.

Sewer lines in Zionsville.

The Town of Zionsville provides sewer service and bills a flat monthly rate per residence. For you that means the street main belongs to the Town's Municipal Sewage Works, while the pipe from your house to that main is generally your concern. Ask the Town where your responsibility ends, because it affects who pays for a repair.

Census figures show 84.2 percent of Zionsville homes are owner-occupied, so most backups land on the homeowner. A cleanout near the foundation is the most useful feature a house can have for this. If your house lacks one, tell us, and we can discuss adding one while the line is open.

How we clear a main sewer line.

We use a heavy drum or sectional cable machine with a 4 inch pipe cutter head, because a small sink cable will not reach or cut in a main. The head chews through paper, grease and thin roots and pulls debris back to the cleanout. For roots or grease that keep returning, we may suggest hydro jetting to wash the walls.

Main sewer cleaning ends when water runs freely. It does not end there for us. Before we pack up, we flush the line with water from a hose to confirm it drains, and we put a camera in if the cable showed anything strange.

Sewer drain cleaning for floor drains and laundry lines.

A floor drain sits at the lowest point of a basement, so it is the first place a blocked main shows itself. A drain that overflows only after heavy rain is a different problem. That points to groundwater or a storm connection, and a sump pump or drainage issue may be involved.

Laundry standpipes and utility sink drains tie into the same main. We cable floor drains from the grate or the trap, and we check that the trap has water in it. A dry trap lets sewer gas into the room, and pouring a quart of water in often ends the smell.

Cleaning, camera or repair.

Cleaning solves a blockage. It does not fix a damaged pipe. This table shows how we decide.

What we find.What it means.Next step.
Paper and soft waste.A one-time clog.Clear with cable, flush, done.
Grease coating the wall.Buildup that returns.Cable, then consider jetting.
Roots at a joint.Open joint feeding roots.Cut roots, camera, plan repair.
Standing water in a dip.A belly in the pipe.Camera, then repair discussion.

The camera is what tells these rows apart. A cable alone cannot see a belly or a crack.

Camera follow-up after the line is clear.

A line that opens after one cable pass has told us little about why it blocked. A short camera run answers it. We look for roots at joints, a sag that holds water, offset joints and scale, and we show you the picture on the screen.

We recommend the camera after any backup that repeats, after roots, and before you buy a house. For the full run from cleanout to street, book a sewer camera inspection. If it shows a break, sewer line repair is the next conversation, and we explain options before any digging.

Storm drain cleaning.

Storm drains are a separate system from the sewer. They carry only rain and runoff from downspouts, driveway drains, yard catch basins and footing drains. When one clogs, you see pooling at a driveway low spot or a window well that fills in rain. We cable and jet these lines the same way, and we pull out leaves, silt and mulch.

The Town of Zionsville is a regulated storm sewer entity under IDEM, and its stormwater ordinance prohibits discharges other than stormwater into the storm drain system. That affects us and you. We do not send wash water or sewage into a storm inlet, and you should not put cleaning water, paint or pool chemicals there. A clear-water sump discharge goes to the storm or yard side, never to the sanitary sewer.
