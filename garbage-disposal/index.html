When a garbage disposal problem is an emergency.

Water running from the bottom of the unit, a steady drip into the cabinet or a sink that fills and will not drain should be handled that day. A leak under the sink can swell the cabinet base and soak flooring, and a night or weekend leak will not stop on its own.

A burning smell, smoke or sparks is different. Switch off the breaker, leave the unit alone and call us. Never reach into a disposal, even when it is off, until you have cut power at the breaker.

What to do while you wait.

These steps are safe for most homeowners and often find the cause.

1

Cut the power.

Switch off the wall switch, then turn off the breaker or unplug the unit under the sink. Water and electricity share this cabinet.

2

Stop a leak.

Place a bucket under the unit and close the sink's hot and cold shut-off valves. If a valve will not close, shut the main water valve.

3

Press the reset button.

The small red button on the bottom of most units pops out when the motor overheats. Wait a few minutes, press it in and try again.

4

Check the breaker and outlet.

A dead disposal with no hum often means a tripped breaker or a tripped GFCI outlet. Reset once. If it trips again, stop.

5

Free a jam with the hex key.

Most units have a hex socket in the center of the bottom. With the power off, turn it back and forth with the hex wrench to free the flywheel.

What goes down a disposal in Zionsville, and where it goes next.

What a disposal grinds ends up in a drain line. The Town of Zionsville provides sewer service and bills a flat monthly rate per residence, so many homes send their kitchen waste to the Town system. Homes on a septic system are served differently, and Indiana local health departments issue septic permits.

Either way, grease, coffee grounds, eggshells, potato peels and fibrous scraps like celery build up in the trap and the line, even though the disposal grinds them. As general guidance, a home on septic should ask its septic service whether a disposal suits the tank, and nothing here replaces that advice.

Signs your garbage disposal needs repair.

How it sounds, and where the water appears, tell us most of what we need.

Hums but does not spin.

The motor has power but the grinder is jammed. A spoon, bone or fruit pit is a common cause.

Silent and dead.

No sound at all points to a tripped breaker, a GFCI outlet, the reset button or a failed switch.

Leaks from the top.

Water at the sink flange means the mounting seal or putty has failed.

Leaks from the side or bottom.

Water at a hose connection can be tightened. Water from the housing itself or the bottom usually means the unit has failed and must be replaced.

Smells even after cleaning.

Food trapped in the grinding chamber or the splash guard causes odors. A deep clean helps, but a leaking seal can also smell.

Grinds slowly or loudly.

A dull or damaged flywheel, or a worn motor, makes grinding weak and noisy. Metal rattling means something hard is inside.

Sink drains slowly.

The clog may be in the trap or the line beyond the disposal, not in the disposal itself.

How we find the cause.

We cut power first, then check the breaker, outlet and reset. If the unit hums, we free the flywheel with a hex wrench and pull out what jammed it, using tongs, not a hand.

For a leak, we dry everything and run water while watching three places in turn, the sink flange, the dishwasher hose and the drain outlet. The wettest spot tells us whether a gasket, a connection or the housing failed. If the sink backs up even when the disposal is working, we trace the clog down the trap and the line.

Garbage disposal replacement and installation.

When a disposal is past repair, we remove it, clean the sink flange and mount a new one. Disposals come in common sizes from about one third to one horsepower, and the right size depends on your household and how you cook. A continuous-feed unit runs while you push scraps in, and a batch-feed unit grinds only with the stopper in place.

A new install has details that people miss. If a dishwasher drains through the disposal, we knock out the plug in the dishwasher inlet, or the dishwasher will not drain. We reconnect the dishwasher hose and the drain trap, check the electrical connection is dry and safe, and run water to test for leaks.

If you are installing a disposal where there never was one, we check the cabinet space, the outlet and the sink drain first. Sometimes that sink has no room, and we say so.

Dishwasher connections and the air gap.

A dishwasher drain hose often ties into the disposal or into an air gap on the countertop. If dishwasher water backs into the sink, or the air gap gurgles or leaks, the cause is usually a clogged hose connection or a knocked-out plug that was never cleared. We reconnect or replace the line, clear the fitting and test a full dishwasher cycle.

Drain and trap condition.

Dishwasher hookup.

Reconnecting the dishwasher line or fixing an air gap is part of the visit.

Electrical.

A disposal needs a switch and a safe outlet or connection. Outlet or wiring problems are handled by a licensed electrician.
