Emergency work or planned work?

Ask one question. Is something getting worse every minute? Running water, sewage in a drain, a flooding basement and a dead water heater in freezing weather all say yes. Those get priority on our 24/7 line, and a same-day appointment may be open.

If the answer is no, you have time to plan. A dripping faucet, a slow drain or an old toilet that still flushes can wait for a scheduled visit. Planned work also lets us talk through options, such as repair versus replacement, without a flood in the background.

Water and pipe problems.

Water in the wrong place is the most common urgent call. Burst pipe repair covers a split or frozen line, and water line repair covers the pipe running from the meter to your house. A bill that jumps with no change in habits suggests leak detection.

Old, repeatedly failing pipe is a different question. When one repair follows another, replacing the pipe run is worth discussing. New fixtures go in under plumbing installation.

Drain and sewer problems.

A single slow drain usually needs drain cleaning, and a stubborn grease or root stoppage may call for hydro jetting. A camera shows what is in the line, which is the point of a sewer inspection.

When the problem is the main line itself, the options are sewer line cleaning or sewer line repair. Sewage in a tub or floor drain is an emergency, so stop using water and call.

Water heaters, fixtures and the basement.

Tank water heaters last 10 to 15 years and tankless units about 20, according to the US Department of Energy. At that age, ask about water heater replacement or a new water heater installation rather than another patch.

Smaller jobs include toilet repair, faucet repair and a garbage disposal. For wet basements, see sump pump service and basement waterproofing. Heating needs go to boiler repair, and a water softener is a planned visit.

Quick checks before you call.

These take a minute and make the call more useful.

1

Locate the main shutoff.

It is usually where the water line enters the house, in a basement or crawl space.

2

Watch the water meter.

If the dial moves with every tap off for two hours, the EPA says you probably have a leak.

3

Check other fixtures.

If only one drain is slow, the clog is local. If several back up together, the main line is the suspect.

4

Note the water heater label.

The tank size and fuel type help us plan the right parts for the visit.

When to pick up the phone.

Call when water will not stop, when sewage comes up, when there is no hot water and the weather is cold, or when you are not sure how serious a problem is. We would rather talk you through a quick check than have you wait out a real emergency.
