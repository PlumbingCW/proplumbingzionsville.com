Plumbing Installation in Zionsville, IN.

If you smell gas, leave first.

A gas smell is not a plumbing appointment. Leave the building right away with everyone inside. Do not flip light switches, use a phone inside, light anything or start a car in an attached garage. From outside, call 911 or your gas utility's emergency number.

The Town of Whitestown names CenterPoint Energy as its natural gas provider. If you are in Zionsville, check your gas bill for the company that serves you, since we do not know which utility that is for your address. Call a plumber only after the utility or fire department says the building is safe.

What counts as plumbing installation.

Installation is new work, as opposed to repairing something that has failed. It includes setting fixtures like toilets, sinks, tubs, showers and laundry boxes. It also includes running new supply and drain lines, adding vents, and connecting appliances such as dishwashers, ice makers and water heaters.

If you are replacing an existing fixture, that is closer to a swap than a build. If you are moving it, adding one or putting a bathroom where none existed, that is a rough-in, and it is a different scale of job. We help you sort out which one you are planning before we price it.

Remodel rough-in: kitchens, baths and basements.

Rough-in is the pipework that goes in before drywall closes the walls. It sets where the drain, vent and supply lines will be, and mistakes are expensive to fix later. A toilet flange a few inches out of position stays that way, and a missing vent can cause slow drains and gurgling in a bathroom you just finished.

Before anything is cut, we confirm fixture locations, cabinet and tub sizes, drain slope and vent paths. Basement bathrooms add the question of where the waste goes. If the fixtures sit below the main sewer line, a sewage ejector pump may lift the waste up, which we install alongside the rest of the work. See our sump and ejector pump page for that equipment.

New construction plumbing.

New construction plumbing happens in stages. Underground drain lines go in before the slab or basement floor is poured. The rough-in follows once the framing is up, and the final trim-out comes after drywall and finishes. Each stage needs to match the builder's plans and the schedule of the other trades.

We work with builders and owner-builders on smaller projects such as additions, garages, accessory buildings and single homes. We do not take large subdivision contracts. The Zionsville Planning & Building Department reviews permits and enforces building code, so confirm the permit before we start.

Gas lines: what we do and what we do not.

Once the building is safe and the utility has cleared you, gas piping inside the house can be inspected, repaired or extended for appliances such as a range, dryer, water heater or furnace. Gas work involves leak testing the pipe with pressure, not just a smell, because low-level leaks are easy to miss.

We do not touch the utility's own lines or meter. Those belong to the gas company. Gas work usually needs a permit, so ask the Zionsville Planning & Building Department what applies and have the permit in hand before we start. Never test for a leak with a flame, and do not cap or move a gas line yourself.

Backflow assemblies.

When supply pressure drops, water already inside a building can be siphoned backward into the public main. A backflow assembly is a valve set that blocks that reverse flow. You will find one on many irrigation systems, fire sprinkler feeds and boiler fills.

We install and replace assemblies, and we can test them. The type required and the testing schedule come from your water utility and the Town, so ask them before you buy a device. Shops and restaurants have extra cases, covered under commercial plumbing.

What affects the price of plumbing installation.

A faucet swap and a basement bathroom share almost nothing, so price ranges would mislead. Here is what moves the number.

Scope of the work.

Swapping a fixture, adding one and building a rough-in are priced differently. Say which one you want.

Access to pipes.

Open framing is cheaper to work in than a finished wall or a slab.

Materials and fixtures.

Pipe type, valves and the fixture you choose all change the total. Premium fixtures can need special rough-in parts.

Permits and inspections.

Some work needs a permit. Confirm with the Zionsville Planning & Building Department.

Related repairs.

Old shutoff valves, corroded supply lines or poor venting may need attention when we open things up.

Insurance rarely pays for planned installation. A standard homeowner policy usually covers sudden and accidental water damage, but not the cost of upgrading fixtures or fixing wear. Check your policy and ask your agent if the work follows a loss.
